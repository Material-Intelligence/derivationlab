claim

Intent ledger for deriving the electric field of a uniformly charged solid sphere in Gaussian units.

why

Compute the static vacuum electric field \(\mathbf E(\mathbf x)\).

step_0002 slot 2, revision 1

claim

For the declared classical vacuum electrostatic problem, spherical symmetry reduces the field to a radial scalar, and Gauss's law then gives \(\mathbf E(\mathbf x)=Q\mathbf x/R^3\) inside the sphere and \(\mathbf E(\mathbf x)=Q\mathbf x/r^3\) outside it.

why

This carries the prescribed uniform charge density through its normalization and enclosed charge to the explicit field in both spatial regions; boundary, limiting, and dependency-ledger closure remain for the next step.

source

The equations \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=4\pi\rho\), \(\nabla\times\mathbf E=0\), the divergence theorem, and electrostatic uniqueness are admitted external primitives in the frozen task. No external references are allowlisted.

derivation

Let \(r=|\mathbf x|\), \(\hat{\mathbf r}=\mathbf x/r\) for \(r>0\), and \(B_r=\{\mathbf x:|\mathbf x|\le r\}\). The prescribed density is
\[
\rho(\mathbf x)=\rho_0\Theta(R-r),\qquad R>0.
\]
Using the Euclidean spherical measure \(d^3x=r'^2\sin\theta\,dr'\,d\theta\,d\phi\), its normalization is
\[
Q=\int_{\mathbb R^3}\rho\,d^3x
 =\rho_0\int_0^R r'^2dr'\int_0^\pi\sin\theta\,d\theta\int_0^{2\pi}d\phi
 =\rho_0\frac{R^3}{3}(2)(2\pi)
 =\frac{4\pi}{3}\rho_0R^3,
\]
so
\[
\rho_0=\frac{3Q}{4\pi R^3}.
\]

SYMMETRY REDUCTION: For any rotation \(\mathcal R\in SO(3)\), define the rotated field
\[
\mathbf E_{\mathcal R}(\mathbf x)=\mathcal R\,\mathbf E(\mathcal R^{-1}\mathbf x).
\]
Ordinary Euclidean divergence and curl are rotation covariant. Since \(\rho(\mathcal R^{-1}\mathbf x)=\rho(\mathbf x)\), \(\mathbf E_{\mathcal R}\) obeys the same electrostatic equations, is regular at the origin, and has the same decay condition at infinity as \(\mathbf E\). By the admitted uniqueness theorem, \(\mathbf E_{\mathcal R}=\mathbf E\), equivalently
\[
\mathbf E(\mathcal R\mathbf x)=\mathcal R\mathbf E(\mathbf x).
\]
At fixed nonzero \(\mathbf x\), every rotation about the axis through \(\mathbf x\) leaves \(\mathbf x\) fixed. The displayed covariance then requires \(\mathbf E(\mathbf x)\) to be invariant under all such rotations, so it has no component perpendicular to \(\mathbf x\):
\[
\mathbf E(\mathbf x)=E(r)\hat{\mathbf r}.
\]
Rotations carrying one point of radius \(r\) to another show that the coefficient depends only on \(r\).

GAUSS LAW FROM THE DECLARED PRIMITIVES: Integrate \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=4\pi\rho\) over \(B_r\) and apply the admitted divergence theorem:
\[
\oint_{S_r}\mathbf E\cdot d\mathbf A
 =\int_{B_r}(\nabla\cdot\mathbf E)d^3x
 =4\pi\int_{B_r}\rho\,d^3x
 =4\pi Q_{\mathrm{enc}}(r).
\]
Here \(S_r=\partial B_r\), its outward oriented area element is \(d\mathbf A=\hat{\mathbf r}\,r^2\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi\), and therefore
\[
\oint_{S_r}\mathbf E\cdot d\mathbf A
 =E(r)r^2\int_0^\pi\sin\theta\,d\theta\int_0^{2\pi}d\phi
 =4\pi r^2E(r).
\]
Thus, for \(r>0\),
\[
E(r)=\frac{Q_{\mathrm{enc}}(r)}{r^2}.
\]
The enclosed charge is explicitly
\[
Q_{\mathrm{enc}}(r)=4\pi\rho_0\int_0^{\min(r,R)}r'^2dr'
=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{4\pi\rho_0r^3}{3}=Q\dfrac{r^3}{R^3},&0<r<R,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{4\pi\rho_0R^3}{3}=Q,&r>R.
\end{cases}
\]
Substitution gives
\[
\boxed{\mathbf E(\mathbf x)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{Q}{R^3}\mathbf x=\dfrac{Qr}{R^3}\hat{\mathbf r},&0<r<R,\\[7pt]
\dfrac{Q}{r^3}\mathbf x=\dfrac{Q}{r^2}\hat{\mathbf r},&r>R.
\end{cases}}
\]
The regular extension to the center is \(\mathbf E(\mathbf 0)=\mathbf 0\), because the interior expression is linear in \(\mathbf x\). Since \(\hat{\mathbf r}\) is outward, \(Q>0\) gives an outward field and \(Q<0\) gives an inward field. No undetermined homogeneous \(C/r^2\) term remains: the flux through every centered sphere was fixed by its actual enclosed charge, regularity holds at the origin, and the exterior solution decays as \(r^{-2}\).

scope

Derived for a static, exactly spherically symmetric, uniformly charged solid sphere in three-dimensional classical linear Maxwell electrostatics in vacuum and Gaussian units, with no external fields, conductors, dielectrics, or additional singular surface charge.

claim

why

source

derivation

FINAL RESULT AND CONDITIONS. With \(r=|\mathbf x|\), \(\hat{\mathbf r}=\mathbf x/r\) for \(r>0\), and \(R>0\), slot 2 found
\[
\rho_0=\frac{3Q}{4\pi R^3},\qquad
\boxed{\mathbf E(\mathbf x)=
\begin{cases}
\mathbf 0,&r=0,\\[3pt]
\dfrac{Qr}{R^3}\hat{\mathbf r}=\dfrac{Q}{R^3}\mathbf x,&0<r<R,\\[7pt]
\dfrac{Q}{R^2}\hat{\mathbf r},&r=R,\\[7pt]
\dfrac{Q}{r^2}\hat{\mathbf r}=\dfrac{Q}{r^3}\mathbf x,&r>R.
\end{cases}}
\]
Here \(\hat{\mathbf r}\) is outward. Hence \(Q>0\) gives an outward field, \(Q<0\) an inward field, and \(Q=0\) the zero field.

BOUNDARY CHECK. Directly,
\[
\lim_{r\to R^-}\mathbf E=\frac{QR}{R^3}\hat{\mathbf r}=\frac{Q}{R^2}\hat{\mathbf r}
=\lim_{r\to R^+}\frac{Q}{r^2}\hat{\mathbf r}.
\]
Thus the normal jump is zero, \(\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot(\mathbf E_{\rm out}-\mathbf E_{\rm in})=0\), consistently with the absence of a surface delta-function charge. The tangential field vanishes on both sides by spherical symmetry.

MAXWELL, SIGN, AND FACTOR CHECKS. In Cartesian components, inside the sphere
\[
E_i=\frac{Qx_i}{R^3},\qquad \partial_jE_i=\frac{Q}{R^3}\delta_{ij},
\]
so
\[
\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=\partial_iE_i=\frac{3Q}{R^3}=4\pi\frac{3Q}{4\pi R^3}=4\pi\rho_0,
\qquad
(\nabla\times\mathbf E)_k=\epsilon_{kji}\partial_jE_i=0.
\]
Outside, for \(r>R\),
\[
E_i=Qx_ir^{-3},\qquad
\partial_jE_i=Q\left(\delta_{ij}r^{-3}-3x_ix_jr^{-5}\right).
\]
Therefore
\[
\partial_iE_i=Q(3r^{-3}-3r^2r^{-5})=0,
\qquad
\epsilon_{kji}\partial_jE_i=0,
\]
because \(\partial_jE_i\) is symmetric in \(i,j\). Continuity at \(R\) prevents a surface delta term in either distributional derivative, and regularity at \(r=0\) prevents a point-charge delta there. The flux is \(4\pi r^2E(r)=4\pi Q_{\rm enc}(r)\); thus the factors and the sign agree with \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=4\pi\rho\).

EXPLICIT LIMITING EXPANSIONS. At the center,
\[
\mathbf E(\mathbf x)=\frac{Q}{R^3}\mathbf x=O(r),\qquad r\to0,
\]
so \(\mathbf E(\mathbf0)=\mathbf0\). Writing \(\delta>0\), the two surface limits are
\[
\mathbf E((R-\delta)\hat{\mathbf r})
=\frac{Q}{R^2}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{R}\right)\hat{\mathbf r},
\]
\[
\mathbf E((R+\delta)\hat{\mathbf r})
=\frac{Q}{R^2}\left(1-2\frac{\delta}{R}+3\frac{\delta^2}{R^2}+O\!\left(\frac{\delta^3}{R^3}\right)\right)\hat{\mathbf r}.
\]
Both have leading value \(Q\hat{\mathbf r}/R^2\), although their radial derivatives differ because the volume density jumps. At large radius,
\[
\mathbf E(\mathbf x)=\frac{Q}{r^2}\hat{\mathbf r}=O(r^{-2})\longrightarrow\mathbf0,
\qquad r\to\infty.
\]
Indeed, throughout \(r>R\) this is exactly, not merely asymptotically, the Gaussian-unit field of a point charge \(Q\) at the origin.

DIMENSIONAL ANALYSIS IN GAUSSIAN UNITS. From the declared equation \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=4\pi\rho\), with \([\nabla]=L^{-1}\), \([\rho]=[Q]L^{-3}\), and \([4\pi]=[\hat{\mathbf r}]=1\), one has \([\mathbf E]=[Q]L^{-2}\). Every factor in the endpoint closes explicitly:
\[
\left[\frac{Qr}{R^3}\hat{\mathbf r}\right]=[Q]L\,L^{-3}\cdot1=[Q]L^{-2}=[\mathbf E],
\qquad
\left[\frac{Q}{r^2}\hat{\mathbf r}\right]=[Q]L^{-2}\cdot1=[\mathbf E].
\]
There is no \(\epsilon_0\) in Gaussian units, and the \(4\pi\) from Maxwell's equation cancels the dimensionless \(4\pi\) area factor of a sphere.
